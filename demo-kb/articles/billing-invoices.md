# Billing & Invoices

How we charge, what shows up on an invoice, and the bits of pricing we still haven't nailed down.

## Pricing tiers

There are three plans — Starter, Pro, and Enterprise.

| Tier | Price | Seats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | $29/mo | 3 |
| Pro | $99/mo | 25 |
| Enterprise | Custom | Unlimited |

The shape of that table follows from who actually shows up to buy.

**Starter exists for the small team that hasn't decided yet.** Three seats is deliberately tight — it covers a founder plus a couple of collaborators, and the moment a fourth person needs in, that's a real signal the team has committed. So the tier isn't really priced to make money; it's priced to be an easy yes.

**Pro is the jump for a team that's committed.** Going 3 → 25 seats is a big leap, and that's on purpose: we'd rather someone move up once and stop thinking about it than creep through four intermediate tiers and feel nickel-and-dimed every quarter. $99 lands in the range most team leads can expense without a procurement conversation, which is the whole point — Pro should never need a meeting.

**Enterprise is custom because the buying process is custom.** Once purchase orders and negotiated terms enter the picture, a list price stops being useful anyway — the number is an output of the contract, not an input. Unlimited seats falls out of the same logic: if we're already talking to a human, seat-counting is just friction we've added for no reason.

## Customers

Two groups, and they behave nothing alike.

**Enterprise buyers** usually need a purchase order before they'll pay an annual invoice, and most teams ask for net-30 terms on their first contract. Neither of those is us being generous — it's how their AP department works, and it isn't negotiable from their side. So the invoice has to come first, the payment shows up a month later, and we plan cash around that gap rather than fighting it.

**Self-serve customers** almost always pay by card at signup. They rarely ask for custom billing unless they outgrow Pro — and "outgrow Pro" is basically the moment they turn into the first group. That's the transition worth watching, because it's where a card-on-file customer suddenly needs an invoice, a PO number, and a person to talk to.
